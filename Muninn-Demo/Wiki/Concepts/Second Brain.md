The Second Brain is a [[Personal Knowledge Management]] methodology developed by [[Tiago Forte]] that provides a systematic approach to capturing, organizing, distilling, and expressing personal knowledge using digital tools. The framework, formalized in Forte's 2022 book "Building a Second Brain," centers on the PARA organizational system (Projects, Areas, Resources, Archives) and the CODE workflow (Capture, Organize, Distill, Express). The core insight is that human biological memory is unreliable for the volume of information modern knowledge workers encounter, so externalizing knowledge into a trusted digital system frees cognitive capacity for creative thinking rather than recall.

The Second Brain concept has deep roots in earlier knowledge management traditions, particularly the [[Zettelkasten]] method developed by Niklas Luhmann. Where Zettelkasten emphasizes atomic, interlinked notes that build emergent structure, the Second Brain methodology focuses more on progressive summarization — layering highlights on top of captured material so that the most essential ideas become immediately visible. Tools like [[Obsidian]], Notion, and Roam Research serve as the technological substrate for Second Brain implementations, with Obsidian's graph view and bidirectional linking making it particularly well-suited for building densely connected knowledge networks.

The emergence of LLM-powered knowledge systems has expanded the Second Brain concept significantly. The [[LLM Wiki]] pattern automates the most labor-intensive parts of the Second Brain workflow — capture, extraction, linking, and organization — by delegating them to a large language model. Projects like [[Muninn]] represent the convergence of Forte's methodology with AI capabilities: the human feeds sources into the system (browsing, bookmarking, reading), and the LLM handles the distillation and cross-linking that Forte's method requires humans to do manually. This shift from human-maintained to LLM-maintained second brains may be the most significant evolution in personal knowledge management since the move from physical to digital notes.
