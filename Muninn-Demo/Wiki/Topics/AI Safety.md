AI Safety is the interdisciplinary field concerned with ensuring that artificial intelligence systems, particularly advanced AI and potential artificial general intelligence, operate in alignment with human values and do not cause catastrophic or existential harm. The field draws from computer science, philosophy, cognitive science, and policy, and has moved from the academic margins to the center of technology discourse as large language models have demonstrated increasingly capable and sometimes unpredictable behavior. Key research areas include alignment (ensuring models pursue intended goals), interpretability (understanding why models produce specific outputs), robustness (ensuring reliable behavior under adversarial or distributional shift conditions), and governance (regulatory and institutional frameworks for AI development).

[[Anthropic]] was founded explicitly as an AI safety company, with [[Dario Amodei]]'s departure from [[OpenAI]] motivated partly by concerns about the pace of capability development relative to safety research. Anthropic's constitutional AI approach — training models to follow explicit principles rather than relying solely on human feedback — represents one concrete methodology for aligning powerful models. [[Geoffrey Hinton]]'s decision to leave [[Google]] in 2023 to speak freely about AI existential risk elevated public awareness of safety concerns, as did [[Ilya Sutskever]]'s founding of Safe Superintelligence Inc. after departing OpenAI. [[Google DeepMind]] maintains a dedicated safety team focused on scalable oversight and interpretability research.

The relationship between AI safety and AI capability is contested. Some researchers argue for pausing development of frontier models until alignment is better understood; others, including Amodei, contend that safety research is most productive when conducted at the frontier, because alignment challenges only manifest at scale. The [[Agent Framework]] paradigm intensifies safety concerns because agents can take real-world actions (executing code, modifying files, sending communications) rather than merely generating text. The [[Open Source AI]] movement adds another dimension: open-weight models enable broader safety research but also make powerful AI accessible to actors who may not prioritize safety.
