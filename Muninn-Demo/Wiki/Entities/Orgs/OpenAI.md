OpenAI is an AI research laboratory founded in December 2015 as a non-profit by Sam Altman, Elon Musk, [[Ilya Sutskever]], Greg Brockman, and others, with the stated mission of ensuring that artificial general intelligence benefits all of humanity. The organization restructured in 2019 to include a capped-profit subsidiary, enabling it to raise the billions of dollars needed to train increasingly large models. Its partnership with Microsoft, which has invested over $13 billion, has been a defining feature of its trajectory. OpenAI's GPT series — particularly GPT-3 (2020) and GPT-4 (2023) — demonstrated the power of scaling [[Transformer Architecture]] models and brought large language models into mainstream awareness.

The organization has been home to many of the AI field's most prominent researchers. [[Andrej Karpathy]] was a founding member and returned for a second stint in 2023. [[Ilya Sutskever]] served as chief scientist and was the driving force behind the scaling philosophy that led to GPT-4. [[Dario Amodei]] served as VP of Research before departing to found [[Anthropic]], taking several colleagues who felt OpenAI was not prioritizing safety sufficiently. The November 2023 boardroom crisis, in which Sam Altman was briefly ousted, underscored the tension between OpenAI's original non-profit mission and its commercial ambitions.

OpenAI's product ecosystem includes ChatGPT (the consumer-facing chatbot that reached 100 million users faster than any application in history), the GPT API platform, DALL-E for image generation, and Whisper for speech recognition. Its competitive position is challenged by [[Anthropic]]'s Claude, [[Google DeepMind]]'s Gemini, and an increasingly capable [[Open Source AI]] ecosystem. The company's influence on [[AI Safety]] discourse has been complex: it has published influential safety research while also being criticized for prioritizing capability advances over alignment work.
