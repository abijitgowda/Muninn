[[NVIDIA]] Corporation (NASDAQ: NVDA) is the world's leading designer of graphics processing units (GPUs) and has become the backbone of the artificial intelligence infrastructure boom. Founded by [[Jensen Huang]], Chris Malachowsky, and Curtis Priem in 1993, the company originally focused on gaming graphics but pivoted decisively toward accelerated computing for data centers beginning around 2016. Its CUDA programming platform, introduced in 2006, gave NVIDIA a decade-long head start in the GPU-compute ecosystem that now dominates AI training and inference workloads.

NVIDIA's data center revenue has grown explosively with the rise of large language models. The A100 and H100 GPUs became the standard hardware for training models like GPT-4 and Claude, and the Blackwell architecture (B100/B200) continues that trajectory. The company's market capitalization surpassed $3 trillion in 2024, briefly making it the most valuable company in the world. Major AI companies including [[Anthropic]], [[OpenAI]], [[Google]], and Meta are among its largest customers, collectively spending billions annually on NVIDIA hardware to power their model training clusters.

The company's dominance in AI infrastructure has made it a bellwether for the entire AI sector. Analyst estimates for NVIDIA's forward revenue are closely watched as a proxy for overall AI capital expenditure trends. Its competitive moat rests on the CUDA software ecosystem as much as the hardware itself — a pattern where developer lock-in reinforces hardware purchasing decisions across the industry.
