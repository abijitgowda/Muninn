Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (NYSE: TSM) is the world's largest dedicated semiconductor foundry, with a market capitalization that has frequently exceeded $700 billion. [[TSMC]] manufactures the advanced chips that power the AI revolution: [[NVDA]]'s GPU architectures (A100, H100, Blackwell), [[AMD]]'s Instinct and EPYC processors, and Apple's M-series silicon are all fabricated on TSMC's leading-edge process nodes. The company commands approximately 60% of the global foundry market by revenue and over 90% of the most advanced sub-7nm production, making it an irreplaceable link in the technology supply chain.

TSM's financial performance tracks closely with AI capital expenditure cycles. The surge in demand for AI training and inference hardware since 2023 has driven TSMC's advanced packaging and 3nm/5nm revenue to record levels. The company's gross margins consistently exceed 50%, reflecting the pricing power that comes from being the sole manufacturer capable of producing chips at the most advanced nodes at scale. [[Jensen Huang]] has publicly described TSMC as [[NVIDIA]]'s most important partner, and the relationship between the two companies is so intertwined that TSMC's capacity allocation decisions directly affect how many GPUs are available for AI training worldwide.

Geopolitical risk is the primary overhang on TSM's valuation. The concentration of advanced chip manufacturing in Taiwan, given cross-strait tensions with China, represents a systemic risk to the global technology ecosystem. TSMC's investments in U.S. fabrication facilities in Arizona (over $40 billion committed) and in Japan are partially designed to mitigate this risk, though analysts note that the most advanced manufacturing is likely to remain in Taiwan for the foreseeable future. The stock's price-to-earnings multiple reflects both the company's dominant competitive position and the geopolitical discount applied by investors pricing tail risks.
