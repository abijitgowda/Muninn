Advanced Micro Devices (NASDAQ: AMD) is a semiconductor company that has transformed under CEO [[Lisa Su]] from a struggling Intel competitor into a diversified computing powerhouse with a market capitalization exceeding $250 billion. [[AMD]]'s product portfolio spans consumer and data center CPUs (Ryzen, EPYC), discrete GPUs (Radeon), and AI accelerators (Instinct MI series), with all leading-edge products manufactured by [[TSMC]]. The company's EPYC server processors have captured significant market share from Intel, now powering a substantial portion of cloud data center infrastructure.

AMD's AI accelerator revenue, while growing rapidly, remains a fraction of [[NVDA]]'s data center business. The Instinct MI300X and upcoming MI400 series target the growing market for LLM inference hardware, where AMD's high-bandwidth memory advantage makes its products competitive for serving large [[Transformer Architecture]] models. Cloud providers including Microsoft Azure, Oracle Cloud, and Meta have deployed AMD Instinct GPUs, though the CUDA ecosystem's dominance means that most AI training workloads still default to [[NVIDIA]] hardware. The ROCm software stack, AMD's open-source alternative, has seen accelerating adoption as the AI community seeks alternatives to vendor lock-in.

The stock's valuation reflects investor expectations about AMD's ability to capture a meaningful share of the AI accelerator market, which analysts project will exceed $200 billion annually by 2027. [[Lisa Su]]'s track record of execution — the Zen architecture turnaround is one of the most successful product pivots in semiconductor history — provides credibility for AMD's AI ambitions. The company's competitive position relative to [[NVDA]] and the overall trajectory of [[GPU Computing]] demand are the primary drivers of AMD's equity story.
