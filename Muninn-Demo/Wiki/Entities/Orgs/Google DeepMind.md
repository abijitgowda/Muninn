[[Google]] DeepMind is the primary artificial intelligence research division of Alphabet (Google's parent company), formed in April 2023 by merging the London-based DeepMind lab with Google Brain in Mountain View. The original DeepMind, founded in 2010 by Demis Hassabis, Shane Legg, and Mustafa Suleyman, was acquired by Google in 2014 for approximately $500 million after demonstrating superhuman performance on Atari games using deep reinforcement learning. Google Brain, founded in 2011, was responsible for foundational contributions including the "Attention Is All You Need" paper (2017) that introduced the [[Transformer Architecture]]. The merger consolidated Google's AI talent under a single organization with combined research output that rivals any institution in the world.

Google DeepMind's portfolio spans fundamental research and deployed products. AlphaFold, which solved the protein structure prediction problem and earned Demis Hassabis a share of the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry, represents the lab's ambition to apply AI to scientific grand challenges. The Gemini family of large language models competes directly with [[OpenAI]]'s GPT series and [[Anthropic]]'s Claude, offering models from the lightweight Nano tier to the full-scale Ultra. Gemini models have pioneered extremely long [[Context Window]] support, with some variants handling over 1 million tokens, enabling applications in document analysis, code understanding, and knowledge synthesis.

The lab has also been central to [[AI Safety]] research within Google, publishing work on scalable oversight, interpretability, and the alignment of general-purpose models. [[Geoffrey Hinton]], who spent a decade at Google Brain before departing in 2023, helped shape the intellectual culture that Google DeepMind inherited. [[Ilya Sutskever]]'s early career at Google Brain, before co-founding [[OpenAI]], further illustrates how deeply intertwined the histories of these organizations are. Google DeepMind's access to Google's vast computational infrastructure — including custom TPU chips that compete with [[NVDA]]'s GPUs — gives it a distinctive hardware advantage in the AI arms race.
