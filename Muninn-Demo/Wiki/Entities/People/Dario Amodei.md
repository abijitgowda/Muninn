Dario Amodei is the CEO and co-founder of [[Anthropic]], the AI safety company behind the Claude family of large language models. Before founding Anthropic in 2021, Amodei served as VP of Research at [[OpenAI]], where he led the team that developed GPT-2 and GPT-3 and contributed to the scaling laws research that demonstrated predictable relationships between model size, data, and performance. His departure from OpenAI, along with his sister Daniela Amodei and several other senior researchers, was motivated by disagreements over the pace of commercialization versus safety research — a tension that has defined the AI industry's development trajectory.

At Anthropic, Amodei has championed "constitutional AI" (CAI), an alignment technique where models are trained to follow a set of explicit principles rather than relying solely on human feedback for every decision. This approach directly shaped how Claude models handle sensitive topics and refuse harmful requests. Amodei has been particularly vocal about the potential for AI to deliver transformative benefits in science, medicine, and education, while simultaneously advocating for robust safety measures. His 2024 essay "Machines of Loving Grace" outlined an optimistic vision of AI-driven progress in biology, climate science, and global development, balanced by clear-eyed assessments of risk.

Amodei's intellectual lineage connects to [[Geoffrey Hinton]]'s foundational work on neural networks and to [[Ilya Sutskever]]'s scaling philosophy at OpenAI. His conviction that [[AI Safety]] requires working at the frontier — rather than pausing development — distinguishes Anthropic's approach from organizations that advocate for moratoriums. The [[Agent Framework]] paradigm, which Anthropic has embraced through products like Claude Code, represents Amodei's belief that useful, deployed AI systems are the most effective testbed for alignment research.
