Jensen Huang is the co-founder, president, and CEO of [[NVIDIA]] Corporation ([[NVDA]]), a position he has held since the company's founding in 1993. Born in Tainan, Taiwan, in 1963, Huang immigrated to the United States as a child, earned degrees in electrical engineering from Oregon State University and Stanford, and worked at LSI Logic and AMD before starting NVIDIA with Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem. Under Huang's leadership spanning three decades, NVIDIA evolved from a niche graphics card company into the dominant supplier of AI computing infrastructure, with a market capitalization that briefly surpassed $3 trillion in 2024.

Huang's most consequential strategic decision was the 2006 launch of CUDA, the programming platform that enabled GPUs to be used for general-purpose computation beyond graphics rendering. This decision, made years before deep learning's breakthrough moment, created the software ecosystem that would later make NVIDIA GPUs the default hardware for AI training. When [[Geoffrey Hinton]]'s student Alex Krizhevsky used NVIDIA GPUs to train AlexNet in 2012, it validated Huang's bet on [[GPU Computing]]. The subsequent explosion in AI model size — from millions to trillions of parameters in [[Transformer Architecture]] models — turned NVIDIA's data center GPU business into a multi-billion-dollar revenue engine. [[Lisa Su]], Huang's distant relative and CEO of competitor [[AMD]], has acknowledged that CUDA's ecosystem advantage is the most significant barrier to challenging NVIDIA's AI hardware dominance.

Huang's public persona — characterized by his signature leather jacket and energetic keynote presentations — has made him one of the most recognized figures in technology. He has been an outspoken advocate for the transformative potential of AI, predicting that accelerated computing will eventually replace general-purpose CPU computing for most workloads. His relationships with AI lab leaders including [[Dario Amodei]] at [[Anthropic]] and executives at [[OpenAI]] and [[Google DeepMind]] position him at the nexus of the AI industry's hardware and software ecosystems. [[TSMC]], which manufactures all of NVIDIA's leading-edge GPUs, is Huang's most critical supply chain partner.
