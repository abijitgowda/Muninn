Ilya Sutskever is a Russian-born Israeli-Canadian computer scientist who has been one of the most influential figures in the deep learning revolution. He completed his PhD under [[Geoffrey Hinton]] at the University of Toronto, where he co-authored AlexNet (2012) with Alex Krizhevsky — the convolutional neural network that won the ImageNet competition by a dramatic margin and catalyzed the modern deep learning era. Sutskever went on to work at [[Google]] Brain, where he contributed to the development of sequence-to-sequence learning with neural networks, a precursor to the [[Transformer Architecture]] that would later dominate the field.

In 2015, Sutskever co-founded [[OpenAI]] alongside Sam Altman, Greg Brockman, and [[Andrej Karpathy]], among others. As chief scientist, he was the primary technical visionary behind OpenAI's strategy of scaling up neural networks — the bet that simply making models larger and training them on more data would yield emergent capabilities. This conviction led to the GPT series and ultimately to GPT-4. In November 2023, Sutskever was involved in the board's brief removal of Sam Altman as CEO, an event that roiled the AI industry. He subsequently departed OpenAI in mid-2024 and founded Safe Superintelligence Inc. (SSI), a startup focused exclusively on building safe superintelligent AI.

Sutskever's intellectual contributions span [[Attention Mechanism]] research, reinforcement learning, and the theoretical understanding of neural network generalization. His emphasis on [[AI Safety]] — particularly the alignment problem for superintelligent systems — has shaped how the field thinks about the risks of increasingly powerful models. [[Dario Amodei]], who worked alongside Sutskever at OpenAI before founding [[Anthropic]], has credited Sutskever with being among the first to take superalignment seriously as a concrete research agenda rather than a philosophical concern.
