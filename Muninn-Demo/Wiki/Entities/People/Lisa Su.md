Lisa Su is the president and CEO of Advanced Micro Devices ([[AMD]]), a position she has held since October 2014. Born in Tainan, Taiwan — the same city as [[Jensen Huang]], her distant relative — Su earned her PhD in electrical engineering from MIT, where she worked on silicon-on-insulator semiconductor technology. Before joining AMD, she held engineering and leadership roles at Texas Instruments, IBM (where she contributed to the development of the PlayStation 3's Cell processor), and Freescale Semiconductor. Her appointment as CEO came at a critical moment: AMD's stock price was below $3, the company was burdened with debt, and many analysts questioned its viability.

Su's turnaround of AMD is widely regarded as one of the most impressive leadership stories in semiconductor history. She made the strategic decision to focus the company's resources on the Zen CPU architecture, which launched in 2017 and immediately closed the performance gap with Intel that had persisted for a decade. Subsequent Zen generations surpassed Intel in both performance and efficiency, enabling AMD's EPYC processors to capture significant data center market share. Simultaneously, Su oversaw the acquisition of Xilinx ($49 billion, closed 2022) to expand AMD's portfolio into adaptive computing and the development of the Instinct MI series of AI accelerators to compete with [[NVDA]]'s dominance in [[GPU Computing]].

Su has positioned AMD as the "credible alternative" to [[NVIDIA]] in the AI hardware market, targeting both training and inference workloads for [[Transformer Architecture]] models. The company's open-source ROCm software stack is central to this strategy, offering organizations like [[OpenAI]], [[Anthropic]], and [[Google DeepMind]] a path away from CUDA vendor lock-in. All of AMD's leading-edge products are manufactured by [[TSMC]], and Su has invested heavily in securing advanced packaging capacity (including CoWoS-style packaging for the MI300X) to ensure supply for the AI acceleration market. The [[AMD (Ticker)]] stock has reflected investor optimism about Su's AI strategy, though the path to meaningful revenue share against NVIDIA remains AMD's defining challenge.
