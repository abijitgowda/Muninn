Geoffrey Everest Hinton is a British-Canadian cognitive psychologist and computer scientist whose work on artificial neural networks laid the foundation for modern deep learning. Born in 1947, Hinton spent decades at the University of Toronto and at [[Google]] Brain advancing techniques that most of the AI community had abandoned. His persistence paid off: the backpropagation algorithm he co-popularized in 1986, Boltzmann machines, and his later work on deep belief networks directly enabled the deep learning revolution that began around 2012 with AlexNet — a convolutional neural network built by his students Alex Krizhevsky and [[Ilya Sutskever]].

In 2024, Hinton was awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics alongside John Hopfield for foundational discoveries enabling machine learning with artificial neural networks. The award recognized that the mathematical frameworks they developed — particularly around energy-based models and associative memory — drew deeply from statistical physics. Hinton has been vocal about the existential risks of AI, leaving Google in 2023 to speak freely about the dangers of the technology he helped create. He has argued that superintelligent AI could emerge sooner than most researchers expect.

Hinton's intellectual lineage runs through much of modern AI: his students and collaborators include Yann LeCun, Ilya Sutskever, and many others who went on to lead major AI labs. The concept of [[Retrieval Augmented Generation]] and related knowledge-grounding techniques can be traced back to his early work on distributed representations — the idea that knowledge should be stored across patterns of activation rather than in discrete symbols.
